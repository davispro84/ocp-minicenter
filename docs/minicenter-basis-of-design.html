A truck-deployable, permit-free, cable-termination-commissioned data hall module: 2 individual OCP ORv3 compute rack cabinets plus an N+1 pair of liquid-cooling CDUs, inside a single 40ft High-Cube ISO shipping container, designed to drop next to stranded/onsite power (substation, generator, solar, gas turbine) with minimal site work. This describes the Standard Tier / original scale — see Section 12 for the current Primary Design (520kW, two-skid).

7. Deployment Concept — Stacked Modules

Corner-post stacking carries the top module's weight to grade — not through the ground module's roof or floor — so heavy switchgear/rectifier equipment belongs at ground level regardless of stacking. Field interconnection is a handful of quick-connects made after the crane sets the top module, not a construction task.

11. Recommended Sequencing

  1. Resolve pressure architecture. Pick Chilldyne-style negative pressure (leak-safe, but a new connector standard) or stay OCP BMQC-compliant (industry-standard, but pressurized-leak risk in an unmanned site). This is the highest-leverage, lowest-cost decision left.
  2. Size heat rejection against real regional design-day data — likely results in a "cold-climate kit" (dry cooler only) and "hot-climate kit" (dry cooler + adiabatic pre-cool stage) rather than one universal design.
  3. Real weight take-off once 1–2 are locked, checked against ISO CSC ratings and federal road-legal gross weight.
  4. Begin Revit/CAD geometry for the container variant — new family placement logic (single-loaded row, no overhead busway), separate from the existing 4-rack pod script.
  5. Structural floor reinforcement design once rack/CDU positions are final.

12. Primary Design (520kW) — Two-Skid Architecture (CURRENT, 2026-09-29)

The Primary Design tier scaled from 2 to 4 racks (260kW → 520kW), using the exact same N+1 Motivair MCDU-25 pair — 625kW capacity against a 520kW load is a ~17% N+1 margin, far more capital-efficient than the original 2-rack design's ~140% margin. This does not fit in one 40ft box: a 5.8m generator plus a ~4.5m compute row leaves only 1.7m for switchgear, a real checked constraint, not a judgment call. Split into two skids, mirroring already-proven industry (Crusoe-style) multi-skid deployment practice rather than inventing a new scheme.

Standard Tier (lower-density rural/edge, 10-30kW/rack) never needed a 600kW-class generator and stays a true single-box design — this split is specific to the Primary tier at 520kW scale.

LOCKED LAYOUT CONSTRAINT (2026-09-29), mandatory for CAD: Skid A's 480VAC distribution (1200A switchgear, busway) must be routed overhead, physically isolated from the underfloor fluid containment pan's drainage path. This is not a preference — it's the entire basis for the two-stage leak-response logic in FMEA 3.1 (leak rope kills pumps only; a separate smoke/fire detector, not the leak rope, trips the main breaker). If busway and fluid piping ever share an elevation or zone, that safety logic breaks. Any CAD layout of Skid A must enforce this separation explicitly, not assume it.
