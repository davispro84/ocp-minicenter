OCP Mini-Center

A drop-and-run, 520kW modular AI compute facility for stranded power — flare gas, curtailed renewables, and decommissioned industrial interconnects.

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY — DRAFT v0.1 — 2026-09-29

1. The Case

Traditional data center buildout takes 24-36 months, gated by grid interconnection queues that most AI compute demand can't wait for. The Mini-Center is built to deploy in days at sites that already have power — just not a facility to use it.

  • Speed: two-skid, flatbed-truckable architecture — crane-set, cable-terminated, commissioned on-site, not built on-site.
  • Compatibility: standard 480V/415V 3-phase AC and OCP BMQC-compliant positive-pressure liquid cooling — supports commercial OEM AI racks (GB200 NVL72-class) with zero proprietary modification. No custom DC bus to qualify hardware against.
  • Density: 520kW of continuous IT load across 4 racks in a 40ft footprint, N+1 CDU redundancy.

2. Physical & Logistical Architecture

Not a vanity single-box concept — a real, weight-checked two-skid split, each unit a standard truckable load.

Site mating: 1200A Cam-Lok power cables (paralleled per phase) and 3" grooved Victaulic fluid couplings, connected across a ~10ft umbilical corridor after crane placement.

3. The Operating Envelope

A bounded, defensible envelope — not an unbounded "works anywhere" marketing claim.

ParameterSpec
Ambient temperature-40°C to +48°C
AltitudeDerate above 4,000 ft
EnclosureIP55/MERV13 overall envelope; IP66/MERV13-16 for the internal electrical/CDU compartment

Hot climate (e.g. Permian Basin / Phoenix-class conditions)

First-principles psychrometric modeling of a hot design day (~43°C dry-bulb, low-20s°C coincident wet-bulb) shows the adiabatic pre-cool stage drops effective dry-bulb air entering the coil to ~24°C. At a realistic 5-8°C coil approach, that supplies the CDU loop at roughly 30-32°C — comfortably under the CDU's 45°C maximum primary supply limit, with real margin to spare.

Cold climate (e.g. Bakken-class conditions)

A structural IMP partition wall thermally and physically isolates the CDU/switchgear compartment. Exterior primary loop runs Dowfrost PG60, protecting to roughly -48°C (verified against Dow's own published glycol freeze-point chart).

Explicitly out of scope: Sahara-grade sandstorm conditions and sub-PG60 extreme cold (below roughly -48°C). Bounding the claim this way is what makes it a real engineering envelope rather than an unenforceable one.

4. Reliability — Unmanned, Remote, and Still Accountable

The design assumes the facility will be hundreds of miles from the nearest technician. Every failure mode below assumes nobody is on-site to intervene.

  • Leak response: welded stainless-steel underfloor containment pan plus zoned liquid-detection rope. A detected leak immediately trips the CDU pumps; a separate fire/smoke detector — not the leak rope — shunt-trips the main breaker, so a fluid event can't cascade into an electrical one.
  • Blackout telemetry: a dedicated 10kVA lithium-ion UPS keeps CDU pumps, trace heating, and the monitoring stack alive independent of the main facility bus. In a total blackout, the site can still report its own fault code via a cellular-primary, satellite-backup uplink.
  • Transit integrity: shock-isolated sub-floor mounting, sized to 2× worst-case dynamic load, protects the compute and cooling payload during unpaved-road transport to remote sites.

5. What the Site Must Provide

The "drop-and-run" model only holds if the site is correctly stubbed up 6-8 weeks ahead of delivery. Full detail in the companion Site Preparation Guide; summary below.

6. Bottom Line

The Mini-Center's architecture, weight budget, thermal envelope, and failure-response logic are locked and internally consistent. The two remaining open items — final generator and adiabatic-cooler model selection — are gated behind vendor engineering submittals already in flight, not open design questions. This is a facility that can go from "site has power" to "site is running AI compute" in the time it takes to pour two concrete pads and drive two trucks.
