This replaces the single simplified block from the Basis of Design (Section 9) with the actual piping path: two separate fluid loops that never mix, connected only through the CDU's heat exchanger.

The one thing to hold onto: there are two loops, not one. The secondary/IT loop lives entirely inside the container and only ever touches the racks and the CDU. The primary/facility loop is the one that actually goes outside to reject heat to the atmosphere. The CDU is the wall between them — it moves heat across, but the fluids never combine.

Why Two Different Glycol Concentrations, Not One

Every prior document in this project referenced "50% PG" as if it applies everywhere. It shouldn't. The secondary loop (inside the container, CDU to racks) never sees true outdoor ambient — it doesn't need aggressive freeze protection. The primary loop is the one exposed outside to the dry cooler, and that's where freeze protection actually has to hold. Recommend explicitly:
  • Secondary loop: DI water or PG25 (matches the OCP BMQC reference fluid already cited) — lower viscosity, better pump efficiency, since it's never exposed to freezing ambient.
  • Primary loop: PG50+ depending on the coldest deployment target — this is the loop the freeze-point chart (still on the open research list) actually needs to size.
This wasn't explicit in any earlier document in this project — flagging it now so it's a stated design decision, not an accidental one-size assumption.
