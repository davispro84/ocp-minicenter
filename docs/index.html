1. The Case

Traditional data center buildout takes 24-36 months, gated by grid interconnection queues most AI compute demand can't wait for. The Mini-Center is built to deploy in days at sites that already have power — flared gas, curtailed renewables, decommissioned industrial interconnects — just not a facility to use it. Standard 480V/415V AC and OCP BMQC-compliant positive-pressure liquid cooling mean it hosts commercial OEM AI racks (GB200 NVL72-class) with zero proprietary modification.

2. Architecture — Two-Skid, 520kW

A generator plus heat rejection plus 4 racks of compute genuinely doesn't fit in one 40ft container at this scale — a real, checked volumetric constraint, not a design preference. Split into two independently truckable skids, mirroring proven industry (Crusoe-style) stranded-power deployment practice.

Full detail, FMEA, and the weight/thermal budget behind these numbers in the Basis of Design and FMEA documents below.

3. How This Was Actually Built

I used Claude Code as an engineering collaborator, not an autocomplete. Every spec came from a real primary source document (OCP Open Rack V3, OCP BMQC manifold spec, vendor cutsheets); every piece of generated Revit geometry was live-tested against a running Revit session before being called done — the automation tool's commit history is 21 real bug-fix commits against real tracebacks, not a single polished drop. When a second AI tool fed in confident-sounding engineering claims, I checked them against primary sources before they reached a document — six fabricated or stale claims were caught and logged rather than shipped. The full process, including what went wrong along the way, is written up honestly in the retrospective below.
  • Primary sources before design decisions — real OCP specs and vendor cutsheets, not assumed numbers
  • Live-testing, not predicted behavior — the automation tool was debugged against an actual running Revit session
  • A verification methodology, with receipts — six AI-proposed claims checked and rejected before entering a document, logged with what made each one suspicious
  • Willingness to reverse a locked decision — the entire power/cooling architecture was rebuilt after a market-fit check found it incompatible with real commercial hardware

4. Current Status

Architecture, FMEA, weight/thermal budgets, and failure-response logic are locked. Real vendor RFIs are drafted and in flight (generator timing, CDU ambient envelope, adiabatic cooler capacity). CAD modeling hasn't started yet. Full phase-by-phase breakdown in the Project Overview document.
