## Phase 1 — Architecture Decisions (blocking, do first)

> **ARCHITECTURE PIVOT (2026-09-28)**: after market-fit research (real: verified against OCP BMQC and Diablo 400 authorship we already had), the Mini-Center drops the Diablo 400 800VDC bus and the Chilldyne negative-pressure loop entirely — commercial OEM AI racks (GB200 NVL72-class) take standard AC and convert to DC internally, so a proprietary DC-bus facility is incompatible with real customer hardware by default, not just a design preference. New baseline: standard 480V/415V 3-phase AC straight to the rack, standard OCP BMQC-compliant **positive**-pressure CDU (Motivair MCDU-25, real cutsheet already in hand), leak risk mitigated via welded stainless containment pans + zoned leak-detection rope (auto-trips CDU pumps + ATS), not via fighting the pressure standard. The **original 4-rack Pod keeps the Diablo 400/800VDC architecture as-is** — it's a deliberate hyperscale-showcase design, not superseded, just not what the Mini-Center uses. See `lib/minicenter_math.py`'s module docstring for the full rationale (this project's own standalone file, split out of the original repo's `engineering_math.py` on 2026-09-29). Items below marked ~~SUPERSEDED~~ are kept for the reasoning trail, not deleted.
>
> **Two-tier product structure adopted**: "Primary Design" (AI-Compute/Stranded-Power — GB200-class, 130-190kW/rack, positive-pressure liquid CDU) and "Standard Tier" (Rural/Disaster-Response — telecom/SCADA/rugged compute, 10-30kW/rack, standard in-row DX or rear-door air cooling). Both share the same container shell, electrical room, FMEA climate kits, and shock-isolated sub-floor — only the compute-room payload differs. This is now the primary design point; Standard Tier is real but secondary.
>
> **TWO-SKID ARCHITECTURE LOCKED, 2026-09-29 (Primary Design tier)**: the 520kW/4-rack scale-up doesn't fit generator + heat rejection + compute in one 40ft box (real volumetric constraint: 4.5m compute row + 5.8m generator leaves only 1.7m for switchgear — checked and confirmed, not a fabricated wall). Resolved by splitting into **Skid A (Compute)**: 40ft HC container — 4× ORv3 racks (520kW), 2× Motivair MCDU-25 (N+1, 625kW), 1200A 480VAC switchboard + ATS, 10kVA Li-ion Aux UPS, IMP partition wall. **Skid B (Mechanical/Power)**: standard flatbed — 600kW-class generator (MD600/SG600 or equivalent) + 550kW adiabatic V-coil fluid cooler, site-mated to Skid A via **Cam-Lok power cables + 3" grooved Victaulic pipe couplings**.
> **Design philosophy, stated explicitly, not just an implementation detail**: this multi-skid, Cam-Lok/Victaulic mating approach deliberately mirrors already-proven industry practice (Crusoe-style stranded-power deployment), not an invented connection scheme — the "single box" framing was an arbitrary holdover from the original hyperscale-showcase concept, not a real requirement of the actual target buyer. Standard Tier (#3, lower density, no 600kW-class generator needed) stays a true single-box design — this split is specific to the Primary tier at 520kW scale.
> **Enclosure flexibility, explicitly noted**: the ISO shipping container was the convenient starting form factor, not a load-bearing design requirement. As long as each skid stays a standard truckable unit and the drop/plug/play deployment model holds, a custom enclosure is an open option for a later iteration, not a constraint to design around now.
> **Still open, not silently resolved**: the "≤10 second NFPA 110" generator start-time claim has now been made and retracted twice (MD350/SG350, then MD600/SG600) — neither cutsheet actually states it. Real timing still requires Power Design Pro or a direct engineering RFI. The adiabatic heat rejection unit's exact vendor/model is still a placeholder (weight/dimensions estimated, not vendor-certified). The weight tracker still needs the heat rejection unit's number added before the road-legal margin claim is final.
>
> **OPERATING ENVELOPE BOUNDED, 2026-09-28 (real market-scoping correction)**: earlier climate simulation tested global extremes (Sahara interior, Oymyakon-class Siberia) and found real failure points (PG60 exceeded at -50°C+, sandstorm-grade dust beyond spec). Rather than engineer around those extremes, correctly recognized that extreme remoteness and existing stranded-power infrastructure are anti-correlated — the real customer (existing substation surplus, flared gas, curtailed renewables, or a decommissioned industrial site's idle grid interconnection) is very unlikely to be sitting in the planet's most extreme locations, precisely because that infrastructure requires some prior industrial development. **Formally bounding the design's stated operating envelope**: -40°C to +48°C ambient, altitude derate above 4,000ft (matches the real Chilldyne cutsheet figure), IP55/MERV13 for standard agricultural/rural dust — deliberately down-scoped from IP66/MERV13-16, not a silent reversion to the earlier NEMA4X/IP55 *equivalence* error (that correction was about a false equivalence claim, not a mandate for IP66 forever). Sahara-grade sandstorm and sub-PG60 extreme cold are explicitly out of scope, not unsolved problems. A bounded, defensible envelope is a stronger engineering claim than an unbounded "works anywhere on Earth" one. Real example target markets: Permian Basin/West Texas (hot, real flared-gas infrastructure already active), Bakken/North Dakota (cold, same), and decommissioned Rust Belt industrial sites (existing grid interconnection, moderate Midwest climate, real redevelopment tailwind) — diligence items for any specific site: confirm the interconnection agreement's capacity is still live (not decommissioned with the plant), and check for brownfield/environmental history.
